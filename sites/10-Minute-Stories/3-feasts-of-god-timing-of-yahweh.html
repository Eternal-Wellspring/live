3. Feasts of God: Timing of Yahweh

Home
1. Paradise of God: Restored
God dwelt with Mankind until they rebelled. God will return so that He may once again dwell in Paradise with us.

His appointed times

Long ago, after God, whose personal name is Yahweh, brought the people of Israel out of slavery in Egypt, Yahweh gave them a calendar of seven yearly feasts. A feast was a holy day or week when the people stopped their ordinary work and gathered to worship. Yahweh called these feasts His own appointed times, set days for holy convocations. 1 Four feasts fell in spring and early summer, and three fell in autumn. This page walks through the feasts in the order of the year. Like the pictures in a stained-glass window, the feasts show what Yahweh is doing in history. The apostle Paul says the feasts are a shadow of things to come, and that the reality is found in Christ. 2 The spring feasts point to what Christ has already done. Spring: what has been fulfilled

Passover

The first feast is Passover. On the night Yahweh freed Israel from Egypt, each family killed a lamb and painted its blood on the doorframe of the house. When Yahweh saw the blood, He passed over that house and spared it. 1  Yahweh did not let the destroyer enter the houses marked with blood. 2  Paul calls Christ the Passover lamb, sacrificed for His people. 3  A sacrifice was an animal killed in worship to deal with sin. People made right with God through Christ's blood will be saved from God's anger through Christ. 4 
1 Exo 12:132 Exo 12:233 1Co 5:74 Rom 5:9

Unleavened Bread

The Feast of Unleavened Bread began the day after Passover and lasted seven days. Leaven is yeast, which makes dough rise. Israel left Egypt in such haste that their bread had no time to rise. 1  Paul uses leaven as a picture of sin that spreads through a whole life. Paul tells believers to clear out the old leaven of malice and wickedness and to keep the feast with honesty and truth. 2 
1 Exo 12:392 1Co 5:7-8

Firstfruits

The Feast of Firstfruits came on the day after the Sabbath, the weekly day of rest. When the harvest began, the priest waved the first sheaf of grain before Yahweh. 1  Christ rose from the dead as the firstfruits, the first of all who have died to be raised, and a sign that the rest will follow. 2 
1 Lev 23:10-112 1Co 15:20

Pentecost (Weeks)

The Feast of Weeks came fifty days after Firstfruits, so the feast is also called Pentecost, from the Greek word for fiftieth. Israel counted the fifty days and then brought a new offering of grain to Yahweh. 1  Long before, Israel had reached Mount Sinai in the third month after leaving Egypt, 2 and Yahweh came down on the mountain in fire, with the sound of a trumpet, to give Israel His law. 3  When the Day of Pentecost came after Jesus rose from the dead, God's Holy Spirit came on Jesus' followers with something like flames of fire. 4  Yahweh had promised that one day He would write His law on His people's hearts. 5  Paul says the Spirit writes on human hearts, not on tablets of stone like the law at Sinai. 6  Scripture does not tie Sinai or the heart-written law to the day of Pentecost in so many words. That link is an Inference.

Autumn: what is still to come

Trumpets

After the summer harvest came the autumn feasts. The Feast of Trumpets fell on the first day of the seventh month, a day of rest and remembrance marked by blowing trumpets. 1  Paul writes that the Lord Jesus Himself will come down from heaven with the sound of God's trumpet. 2  At the last trumpet the dead will be raised, never to decay again. 3  Scripture does not name these events as the fulfilment of the feast. The link is an inference drawn from the trumpet.
1 Lev 23:242 1Th 4:163 1Co 15:52

Day of Atonement

Ten days later came the Day of Atonement. Atonement means covering sin so that people can be made right with God. On this one day each year, the high priest went behind the veil, the curtain that closed off the innermost room of God's house, and the high priest always took blood with him. 1  The high priest also confessed Israel's sins over a live goat, and the goat carried those sins away into the wilderness. 2  Christ entered the Most Holy Place once, for all time, with His own blood. 3  The prophet Zechariah says Israel will one day look on the One they pierced and will mourn, 4 and a fountain will be opened to wash away their sin. 5  Zechariah does not name the Day of Atonement. The link is drawn from the mourning and cleansing.
1 Heb 9:72 Lev 16:21-223 Heb 9:124 Zec 12:105 Zec 13:1

Tabernacles

Five days later came the Feast of Tabernacles, the last feast of the year. For seven days Israel lived in booths and rejoiced before Yahweh. 1  The booths reminded Israel of the shelters they lived in after Yahweh brought them out of Egypt. The Gospel of John says that Jesus, the Son of God, became a human being and lived among people, like God pitching His tent among them. 2  In the coming kingdom, the nations will go up to Jerusalem year by year to worship the King and keep the Feast of Tabernacles. 3  At the very end of the Bible, a loud voice from heaven announces that God now makes His home with people. 4 
1 Lev 23:40-422 Jhn 1:143 Zec 14:164 Rev 21:3

The calendar is the story

So Israel's calendar tells the whole story in a single year. The year opens with a lamb's blood that saves God's people from death in Egypt, 1 and the year closes with a feast that remembers God bringing His people out to live with Him. 2  The Bible runs the same way, from God's rescue of His people to God living with His people forever on a renewed earth. 3 
1 Exo 12:132 Lev 23:42-433 Rev 21:1-3
