Two families of the sons of God

This page tells the story of God's family, from the beginning of the Bible to the end. The Bible uses the phrase sons of God for two groups. The first group is heavenly beings who stand in God's presence. 1 The second group is people on earth whom God takes as His own children. 2 The story follows both groups. Some of the heavenly sons of God rebel, the first son of God on earth falls, and God then works through history to gather a family that will live with Him forever.Before God made the world, God had already chosen a people to become His sons. 3 When God laid the foundations of the earth, the heavenly sons of God shouted for joy. 4 God then formed the first man, Adam, and the Gospel of Luke calls Adam a son of God. 5 So God had one family of sons in heaven and the beginning of another on earth. God's plan was to bring His whole family in heaven and on earth together under Christ. 6

Eden: a son of God and the serpent rebel

God, whose personal name is Yahweh, placed Adam in a garden called Eden to work it and look after it. 1  A serpent in the garden contradicted God and told the woman, Eve, that eating the fruit of the one forbidden tree would make her like God. Eve ate the fruit, Adam ate it too, and both of them hid from Yahweh God. 2  Yahweh sent Adam and Eve out of the garden, and heavenly guards called cherubim blocked the way back to the tree of life. 3  So the first son of God on earth lost his home with God.Yahweh did not leave the matter there. Yahweh told the serpent that a Seed, a descendant of the woman, would one day crush the serpent's head, while the serpent would wound the Seed's heel. 4  The book of Revelation later names the serpent as the Devil, also called Satan. 5  The apostle Paul says the promised Seed is Christ, who was born of a woman. 6  Genesis itself does not name the Seed, so the reader has to wait for the rest of the story.
1 Gen 2:152 Gen 3:5-83 Gen 3:23-244 Gen 3:155 Rev 12:96 Gal 3:16 Gal 4:4

The Flood: the sons of God rebel

As the number of people on earth grew, the heavenly sons of God saw the daughters of men and took any of them they wanted as wives. 1  Genesis tells this in only a few words. The apostle Peter adds that some angels sinned and that God cast those angels down into darkness to wait for judgement. 2  Jude adds that some angels left the place God had given them. 3  Neither Peter nor Jude names Genesis 6, so linking their words to Genesis 6 is a conclusion drawn from the verses. Babel: the nations are given to the sons of God

After the Flood, people gathered on a plain and began to build a city with a tower, so that they could make a name for themselves. 1  Yahweh confused their language so that they could no longer understand one another, and Yahweh scattered them over the whole earth. 2  Moses later sang that the Most High gave each scattered nation its land and set the borders of the nations by the number of the sons of God, but Yahweh kept the people of Jacob, Israel, as His own. 3  The ESV, an English translation, reads "sons of God" in this verse, following a Dead Sea Scroll and the Septuagint, an ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament. The NKJV follows the standard Hebrew text, which reads "children of Israel". Moses warned Israel not to worship the host of heaven, which Yahweh had given to the other nations. 4  So the other nations were handed over to lesser powers, and only Israel was kept as Yahweh's own. Later in the story the nations are brought back.
1 Gen 11:42 Gen 11:93 Deu 32:8-94 Deu 4:19

God creates His own nation of sons

Yahweh then began to build a family of sons on earth. Yahweh called a man named Abraham and promised that every family on earth would be blessed through him. 1  Abraham's descendants became the nation of Israel. After Israel had been slaves in Egypt, Yahweh brought them out and told them that they would be His own treasured people and a nation of priests, 2 a people who worshipped no other god. 3  Even so, Israel turned away from Yahweh and offered sacrifices, animals killed as gifts of worship, to demons and to foreign gods. 4  Because of this, the glory of Yahweh, the visible sign of His presence, left the temple in Jerusalem. 5  The northern kingdom of Israel was taken captive to Assyria, 6 and the southern kingdom of Judah was later taken captive to Babylon. 7  God's family of sons on earth had failed, just as Adam had failed.
1 Gen 12:1-32 Exo 19:5-63 Exo 20:34 Deu 32:175 Ezk 10:186 2Ki 17:67 2Ki 25:11

The sons of God are judged

While God's sons on earth failed, the heavenly powers over the nations failed too. Psalm 82 describes a scene in heaven. God takes His place in the congregation of the mighty, an assembly of heavenly beings, and judges the gods there. 1  The word for God here is Elohim in Hebrew, not Yahweh. God calls these gods sons of the Most High, yet tells them that they will die like men. The psalm ends by asking God to rise up and judge the earth, because all the nations belong to God. 2  So the heavenly sons of God who were given the nations at Babel will lose them, and the nations will return to God.
1 Psa 82:12 Psa 82:6-8

The Son of God brings many sons

Then the promised Seed came. The Son of God was born as a human being, and people saw His glory as the Father's only Son. 1  This Son is Jesus, also called Christ, a title that means the anointed one, the King God had promised. God's aim was to bring many sons to glory. God made Jesus perfect through suffering, and Jesus is glad to call those sons His brothers. 2  The Son of God came to undo the Devil's work. 3  When Jesus died on the cross, He stripped the ruling spiritual powers of their weapons. 4  The serpent's end is now certain. God will soon crush Satan under the feet of God's people, 5 and the Devil will finally be thrown into the lake of fire. 6  The promise made in Eden, that the Seed would crush the serpent's head, is being kept.
1 Jhn 1:142 Heb 2:10-113 1Jn 3:84 Col 2:155 Rom 16:206 Rev 20:10

Adopted sons of God

What Adam lost, Christ gives back. Everyone who receives Christ is given the right to become a child of God. 1  All who believe are sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus, and in Christ it makes no difference whether a person is a Jew or a Greek. 2  This includes you, the reader. The Father loves His people so much that He calls them His children. 3  God's Holy Spirit makes believers God's adopted children, so believers call God Abba, which means Father. As God's children, believers will also inherit what God has promised. 4  God marks believers with the promised Holy Spirit, who guarantees that inheritance. 5  Until Christ returns, believers struggle against principalities and powers, the rebel rulers in the heavenly places. 6  Yet none of these powers can cut believers off from God's love in Christ Jesus. 7 
1 Jhn 1:122 Gal 3:26-283 1Jn 3:14 Rom 8:15-175 Eph 1:13-146 Eph 6:127 Rom 8:38-39

The revealing of the sons of God

The adopted sons of God are not yet seen for who they are. All creation is waiting eagerly for the day when God reveals His sons. 1  When Christ appears, believers will be like Christ. 2  Those who stayed faithful to Christ will live and reign with Him. 3  The prophet Daniel saw that God would give the kingdom to His holy people. 4  God gave Adam rule over the earth, and Adam lost it. 5  Now the sons of God in Christ receive the kingdom.
1 Rom 8:192 1Jn 3:23 Rev 20:44 Dan 7:275 Gen 1:28

One family of the sons of God

The story ends where God always meant it to end. God makes His home with His people on a renewed earth, 1 and His people see His face. 2  The tree of life, which Adam lost in Eden, stands again in God's garden, called Paradise. 3  God's whole family in heaven and on earth takes its name from God, 4 and that family is gathered into one in Christ, just as God planned before the world began. 5
