Lost and given back

This page sets the beginning of the Bible beside its end. In Genesis, the first book of the Bible, people lose one good thing after another: their home with God, a shared language, and life itself. In Revelation, the last book of the Bible, God gives each of those things back, and often gives back more than was lost. Each section below tells one loss, then tells how God reversed it.

The garden lost, and the garden regained

Fall

In the beginning God placed the first man and woman, Adam and Eve, in a garden called Eden, where the tree of life grew. 1  After Adam and Eve disobeyed God, God sent them out of the garden, and heavenly guards called cherubim, with a flaming sword, blocked the way back to the tree of life. 2 
1 Gen 2:8-92 Gen 3:23-24

Reversal

At the end of the Bible the way is open again. Jesus promises that everyone who stays faithful to the end will eat from the tree of life in the Paradise of God. 1  The leaves of the tree of life bring healing to the nations, 2  and the gates of God's city are never shut. 3  The garden that was guarded is now open.
1 Rev 2:72 Rev 22:23 Rev 21:25

Babel: language confused, and Pentecost: language heard

Fall

After the great flood, the people of the earth all spoke one language. At a place called Babel they gathered to build a tower and make a name for themselves. God, whose personal name is Yahweh, confused their language so that they could not understand one another, and then Yahweh scattered them across the earth. 1  The Most High, a title of God as ruler over all nations, set the borders of the scattered nations according to the number of the sons of God, heavenly beings who serve God. 2  The ESV, an English translation, reads "sons of God" in this verse, following a Dead Sea Scroll and the Septuagint, an ancient Greek translation. The NKJV follows the standard Hebrew text and reads "children of Israel". The divided nations were given the host of heaven as their portion, 3  and the nations served their own gods. 4 
1 Gen 11:4-82 Deu 32:83 Deu 4:194 Deu 12:2

Reversal

Many centuries later the scattering began to be undone. Pentecost was a yearly feast, when Jews from many nations gathered in Jerusalem. At Pentecost, after Jesus had risen from the dead and returned to heaven, God's Holy Spirit came on Jesus' disciples. Something like flames of fire rested on the disciples, and visitors from many nations each heard the disciples speaking in the visitors' own languages. 1  At Babel nobody could understand anyone else. 2  At Pentecost everybody understood. Through the prophet Zephaniah, Yahweh had promised to give the peoples pure speech so that they could all call on His name. 3  At the end of the story, a great crowd from every nation and language stands together before God's throne. 4 
1 Act 2:3-62 Gen 11:73 Zep 3:94 Rev 7:9

Sinai and Pentecost: three thousand

Fall

After God brought Israel out of slavery in Egypt, Israel camped at Mount Sinai, where God gave them His law. While Moses, their leader, was on the mountain, the people made a golden calf and worshipped it as a god. 1  About three thousand men died that day as judgement. 2 
1 Exo 32:82 Exo 32:28

Reversal

At Pentecost, when God gave His Spirit, about three thousand people believed and were added to the followers of Jesus that day. 1  The law given at Sinai ended that day with three thousand dead. The Spirit given at Pentecost began with three thousand given new life. Paul explains that the written law brings death, but the Spirit gives life. 2 
1 Act 2:412 2Co 3:6

A name for themselves, and His name

Fall

The builders at Babel wanted to make a name for themselves, so that people would remember and honour them. 1 
1 Gen 11:4

Reversal

Jesus did the opposite. Jesus did not seek a name for Himself but humbled Himself to the point of death on a cross, and God gave Jesus the name above every name. 1  At the end of the Bible, God's servants carry God's name on their foreheads. 2  The people who tried to make their own name were scattered, and the people who carry God's name are gathered.
1 Php 2:8-92 Rev 22:4

A tower up to heaven, and a city down from heaven

Fall

The builders at Babel wanted their tower to reach up into the heavens. 1  People were trying to climb up to God by their own effort.
1 Gen 11:4

Reversal

At the end of the Bible the direction is reversed. The New Jerusalem, God's holy city, comes down out of heaven from God. 1  People could not climb up to heaven, so God comes down to them.
1 Rev 21:2

One man's disobedience, and one Man's obedience

Fall

The apostle Paul explains that sin came into the world through one man, Adam, and that death came with sin, so death spread to everyone. 1 
1 Rom 5:12

Reversal

Jesus is the second one man in Paul's story. Through the obedience of Jesus, many people are made right with God. 1  Just as all people die because of Adam, so all who belong to Christ will be made alive. 2 
1 Rom 5:192 1Co 15:22

Sons of God who rebelled, and sons of God revealed

Fall

Before the great flood, heavenly beings called the sons of God took wives from among women on earth, 1  and the earth was filled with violence. 2 
1 Gen 6:22 Gen 6:11

Reversal

In the New Testament the title sons of God is given to a new family. All who believe are sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus. 1  All creation is waiting eagerly for the day when God reveals these sons, and on that day creation itself will be set free. 2  The sons of God who rebelled filled the earth with violence. The sons of God in Christ will see the earth set free.
1 Gal 3:262 Rom 8:19-21

The nations disinherited, and the nations inherited

Fall

After Babel, the Most High, a title of God as ruler over all nations, divided the nations according to the number of the sons of God and kept the people of Jacob, Israel, as His own portion. 1  The ESV reads "sons of God" in this verse, following a Dead Sea Scroll and the Septuagint, an ancient Greek translation. The NKJV follows the standard Hebrew text and reads "children of Israel". So every nation except Israel was handed over to other powers.
1 Deu 32:8-9

Reversal

The nations are not lost forever. Psalm 82 ends by asking God to rise up and judge the earth, because all the nations belong to God. 1  At the end of the Bible, the nations walk in the light of the New Jerusalem. 2  The nations that were handed over at Babel are welcomed home.
1 Psa 82:82 Rev 21:24

The curse, and no more curse

Fall

After Adam disobeyed, God cursed the ground. The ground would grow thorns and thistles, people would toil for their food, and every person would die and return to dust. 1 
1 Gen 3:17-19

Reversal

Jesus, the King God promised, wore a crown of thorns when soldiers mocked Him before His death. 1  Paul says Jesus became a curse by being hung on a tree, the cross, to free His people from the curse of the law. 2  At the end of the Bible there is no more curse 3  and no more death. 4 
1 Mat 27:292 Gal 3:133 Rev 22:34 Rev 21:4

Glory departed, and God with us

Fall

In Eden, God walked in the garden where the first people lived. 1  Later God lived among Israel in the temple in Jerusalem. When Israel kept worshipping idols, the glory of Yahweh, the visible sign of God's presence, left the temple. 2 
1 Gen 3:82 Ezk 10:18

Reversal

Jesus was called Immanuel, which means God with us. 1  At the end of the Bible, God makes His home with people on a renewed earth, 2  and they see His face. 3 
1 Mat 1:232 Rev 21:1-33 Rev 22:4

Eden to Eden

So the story ends where it began, only better. The story began with Yahweh God walking in a garden where the first man and woman lived. 1  The story ends with God living among His people forever, 2  with the tree of life growing in the middle of God's city. 3 
1 Gen 3:82 Rev 21:33 Rev 22:2
